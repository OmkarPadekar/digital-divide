The digital divide is not one number.

Having a signal is only the first layer. Researchers track four layers together: infrastructure, digital literacy, affordability and socio-economic outcomes. A place can score high on one and still be left behind.

Physical infrastructureNetworks, coverage and devices exist Digital literacyPeople know how to use them safely and well AffordabilityPeople can pay for data and devices Socio-economic outcomesBeing online improves learning, work and income

Four layers, each with its own indicators

Each layer asks a different question. Together they show where access breaks down.

Physical infrastructure

Is the connection there?

  • Broadband and mobile coverage
  • Network speed and reliability
  • Device ownership per household

Digital literacy

Can people use it well?

  • Basic operating skills
  • Online safety and privacy
  • Skills for work and learning

Affordability

Can people pay for it?

  • Data cost as a share of income
  • Price of a basic device
  • Cost of repairs and upgrades

Socio-economic outcomes

Does being online change lives?

  • Access to online education
  • Income and job opportunities
  • Use of public and financial services
