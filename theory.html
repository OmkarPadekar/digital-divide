Theory & context

The divide is not a gap. It is a chain.

A person can live under a strong signal and still be excluded from the benefits of being online. The theory behind this framework follows the chain from availability to agency and impact.

01 · Start with access

Availability is necessary, but never sufficient.

The first version of the digital divide was often framed as a simple binary: connected or not connected. That question still matters, especially where networks, devices and electricity are unreliable. But it does not tell us whether the connection is usable or useful.

A more complete view asks what happens after the signal arrives. Can someone afford to stay online? Can they navigate a service safely? Do they have the time, confidence and support to turn access into opportunity?

“Being connected is a condition for participation — not proof of participation.”
02 · The four layers

Think of inclusion as a sequence.

01

Access

A network, a device and a connection that works when it is needed.

02

Capability

The skills, confidence and safety practices to use digital tools.

03

Affordability

A price that a household can sustain, not just a one-time sign-up.

04

Agency

Real gains in education, work, health, services and participation.

03 · Why it matters

Measure the point where progress gets stuck.

The layered score is not intended to reduce a complex social issue to one perfect number. It is a conversation starter: a way to notice when infrastructure is moving faster than skills, affordability or outcomes.
